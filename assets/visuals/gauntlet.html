/co-founder · gauntlet · advisor board · radar

The board flags nothing outside its territory.

Five specialists, each reading the founder's own files before opining. Generic personas give generic advice; these lenses are loaded with this business, and overlap is how panels degenerate into five copies of the same opinion.

how to read this radar

The axes are territories, not scores. Each spoke is one lens and the boundary of what it is allowed to flag. The rings are the reporting cap — three findings maximum per lens — and the points where two axes meet are where corroboration upgrades a finding. Nothing here is plotted from data.

step 3 · the deep tier · big bets only

The lenses run in parallel where the harness allows it, otherwise as sequential passes with a fresh eye per lens.

before any lens opines

What every lens reads first.

From the charter: the opening business description, the laws, the glossary. From the vault: the vision file, plus the lens's own files.

A missing or empty file is recorded as no vault receipt found for this lens, and the lens proceeds from the available facts. It never invents a finding to fill the gap.

report format · per lens

Three findings maximum, one line each.

The line carries the issue, the receipt it rests on, and severity. A lens with nothing real to flag reports clear: padding a report to look thorough is a failure.

Run the durable claim admission gate on each finding first: reopen and quote the cited passage, or show the calculation. An interpretation without that support is a question, not a finding.

merge rules · where the axes meet

Corroboration upgrades.

  1. Deduplicate by issue, never by wording.
  2. The same issue flagged independently by two lenses becomes SERIOUS regardless of individual ratings, and gets said first.
  3. Order the merged list: corroborated issues, then SERIOUS, then LOW.
  4. A finding whose receipt cannot be named is demoted to a question.
  5. The merged findings feed back as the gauntlet's final round.

step 4 · the verdict

It survives or it dies.

Every SURVIVES or DIES verdict is a consequential go/no-go recommendation, whatever the bet's size. Both receipt legs must exist: an internal receipt and a checked external source.

A missing leg replaces it with the canonical parked shape, plus the recall or research action. Enthusiasm cannot make it a go.

01

Customer

owns

Willingness-to-pay evidence and buyer-language fit: would the actual named customer want this, from this founder, judged by what they have already paid for and how they speak.

does not flag

Price math, floors, margins, brand tone, operational load.

02

Cash

owns

All money math: unit economics, cash timing, runway impact, margins, and price-floor law violations. Whether the math works at the founder's real volume.

does not flag

Demand or desirability, or legal exposure of money promises.

03

Brand

owns

Positioning, naming, promise clarity; contradictions with brand-specific laws and past positioning decisions; whether the idea cheapens or confuses the promise.

does not flag

Whether it sells, price-floor laws, or the always-ask law.

04

Ops

owns

Can one person actually run this alongside what is already executing; what breaks at 2x volume; which existing commitment this silently cannibalizes.

does not flag

Whether it is worth running. That verdict belongs upstream.

05

Risk

owns

The irreversible parts, the worst plausible outcome and its cost, and legal or trust-damaging exposure: public claims, guarantees, refunds, contracts, scarcity that is not real.

does not flag

Ordinary execution difficulty, or routine money math.
